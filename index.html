The model

base = 524.392 + 0.04665 × displacement (kg) − 0.04871 × crew weight (kg) − 0.8683 × RM rated (kg·m) − 0.6038 × mainsail rated (m²) pair − 0.1148 × headsail rated (m²) pair − 0.1139 × spinnaker (m²) pair − 0.0361 × headsail flying (m²) pair APH ToD = base × (1 + RS Assessment / 100)

Terms marked pair are not fitted by regression. Each is read directly from two real certificates that differ only in that input (see "Slopes measured from certificate pairs"). The first three terms are an ordinary regression across all 22 certificates, after removing the pair terms and the RS Assessment.

ToT is exact. APH ToT = 600 / ToD held on every certificate checked, to rounding.

APH is a fixed average of the certificate's own table. On all 28 certificates pulled, APH ToD equals a fixed wind-weighted average of the "All purpose" time-allowance row, to within 0.07 s/NM. The weights peak at 12 kt (about 0.31) and are near zero at 4 and 24 kt.

What the certificate tables revealed

new, 2026-10-18

The earlier version claimed about 2 s/NM of scatter that no input could explain: Pomorski ST, ADRENALINE and BANSHEE had identical displacement, crew, sails and RM but rated 480.4, 482.5 and 480.6. Diffing their full certificates and time-allowance tables found the inputs that explain the gap:

RS Assessment is printed on every certificate (−0.127% to −0.280% in this fleet, 0.000% on one Club certificate). It scales every time allowance by the same percentage. That's worth up to about 0.7 s/NM between boats. ORC documentation defining it wasn't found, so this is what the tables show, not what the rule says.

A flying headsail (Pomorski ST, 71.90 m²) cuts light-air reaching allowances sharply: 680.5 → 593.7 s/NM at 90° in 4 kt. Net effect is about −2.6 s/NM on APH.

Blank mast weight and mast CG (BANSHEE, Club certificate) changed only the upwind rows, increasingly with wind speed: −13.4 s/NM on the beat at 24 kt. That pattern means the VPP rated it stiffer. Certificates with these fields blank (BANSHEE, PLAYLIST) are left out of the fit.

Slopes measured from certificate pairs

Mainsail, −0.604 s/NM per m². F1's filed certificate vs. test certificate 1: P shortened 13.420 → 13.320 m, rated main 41.87 → 41.04 m², everything else identical. APH 497.3 → 497.8.

Spinnaker, −0.114 s/NM per m². F1 test 1 vs. test 2: spinnaker 121.03 → 120.15 m², APH 497.8 → 497.9. The shape ratio also changed (99% → 110%), so this is the effect of that sail swap, not a pure area slope.

Headsail, −0.115 s/NM per m². ADRENALINE vs. Spitfire: same displacement, crew and RM; headsail 23.58 → 22.23 m². The mainsail and spinnaker differences in that pair are removed using the two slopes above. Mischief, not used for any slope, checks it: actual +1.70 s/NM vs. ADRENALINE, model +1.45.

Flying headsail, −0.036 s/NM per m². Pomorski ST vs. ADRENALINE: identical except the 71.90 m² flying headsail. Only one size has been seen, so treat it as on/off.

Each slope rests on a single pair. A one-change-at-a-time test certificate for each input would confirm them.

How much to trust it

±1.4 s/NM on a boat the model has never seen. This is leave-one-out error over the 16 boats that were not used to measure any slope. 9 of the 16 land within 1 s/NM. The worst is WICKED at −3.1 (770 kg crew), then GEMINI II at −2.4 (crew is ORC's default weight, not declared) and Crazy Diamond at +2.2 (RM estimated, not measured).

F1, predicted without F1 in the regression: 497.15 vs. the filed 497.3, 497.65 vs. 497.8 and 497.75 vs. 497.9, all within 0.15 s/NM. With no F1 data used anywhere, not even for the two slopes, the error is 0.3–0.8 s/NM. F1 is the heaviest boat in the fleet (1,961 kg), so this is extrapolation that happened to hold.

Not modelled: code zeros and reaching kites (SHW/SFL < 85%), mast weight and CG, carbon rudder, non-spinnaker and double-handed certificates. On the reaching legs some of these matter, so their certificates carry more error.

This is not the ORC VPP. It fits the boat-to-boat differences within one class that shares one hull offset file (MELGES32.OD.OFF), which is what makes it possible and also what limits it to this class.
